# Piccolo Is Aura Farming and His Friends Are Paying the Price

We need to talk about Piccolo.

Not the good Piccolo. Not the "I sacrificed myself for Gohan" Piccolo. I'm talking about the Piccolo who, in the middle of an active life-or-death battle, decides it's the perfect moment to plant his feet, clench his fists, and slowly power up while his aura rips his cape to shreds in ultra slow motion.

Meanwhile his teammate is getting stomped into the ground four feet away.

This isn't fighting. This is aura farming. And it's a real problem in this show.

---

## What Is Aura Farming, Exactly

Aura farming is when a character stops doing anything useful in a fight so they can generate maximum aesthetic damage. Wind picks up. Rocks start floating. Hair does something illegal. The music swells like the composer just remembered he's getting paid per second of screaming.

Meanwhile, zero punches have been thrown. Nobody has been saved. A teammate is currently eating dirt.

But it looks incredible, so nobody in the show ever seems to mind.

---

## The Actual Problem With This

Here's the thing nobody talks about: aura farming has a cost, and that cost is your friend's face.

Every single time someone decides "now is the moment I power up dramatically," there is a teammate somewhere getting absolutely worked over by the bad guy, just waiting for the light show to finish.

Vegeta does this constantly. Krillin gets thrown into a mountain, and Vegeta's just standing there going "hmph" while his energy slowly builds like a kettle. Krillin, my man, is unconscious. The kettle can wait.

Gohan has done this too. Cell is about to delete the planet, and Gohan's response is to stand still and power up while everyone screams his name from the sidelines like that's going to speed anything up. It never does. It just adds a extra two minutes of standing.

---

## Why They Keep Doing It Anyway

If you actually think about it in-universe, there might be a reason.

Half-powered attacks in this show routinely just... don't work. A rushed punch bounces off the villain like nothing happened. So the logic becomes: don't bother attacking until you're at absolute maximum, even if that means your friend takes six more hits while you're getting there.

It's less "main character main-charactering" and more "everyone in this show has unknowingly agreed that one guy's glow-up is worth more than three guys' ribs."

Nobody's ever called a team meeting about this. Nobody's ever said "hey, maybe power up during a lull, not while Yamcha's getting folded in half." It's just accepted. It's tradition now.

---

## Piccolo Specifically Needs To Answer For This

Piccolo will see his student getting thrown around like a ragdoll and decide this is the moment for a slow, dramatic power-up walk toward the camera. Cape torn. Eyes narrowed. Ground cracking beneath him.

Gohan, twelve years old, still airborne from the last hit: "..."

And somehow it always works out. The power-up finishes right as things get truly desperate, Piccolo shows up, one punch ends it, and everyone forgets the ninety seconds of pure negligence that came before it.

That's the real secret of this show. The aura farming always pays off, right at the last possible second, which is exactly why nobody in the cast has learned their lesson and everybody keeps doing it. Why help your friend when you can look devastating in six more seconds.
